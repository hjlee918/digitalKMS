## The claim in one sentence
A note collection is worth what its **link density** is worth, not what its note count is. Every rule below therefore exists to force the question "what does this connect to?"

## Four kinds of note
Distinguished by the `type::` property, not by folder. Folders trap a note in one place; the point of a zettelkasten is that one note sits in several contexts at once.

- `fleeting` — a passing thought. Goes in the journal. **Promote or discard within 48 hours.**
- `literature` — a summary of something read, rewritten in my own words. Verbatim text belongs in quote blocks only.
- `permanent` — one finished thought. One claim per note. Links to at least one other note.
- `structure` — a map of content (MOC) threading several permanent notes together. Written after a topic has matured, not before.

## What makes a permanent note
1. The title states the claim. Not "cognitive load" but "cognitive load comes from the working-memory bottleneck".
2. It stands on its own six months later, without surrounding context.
3. It carries at least one `[[link]]`. A permanent note with no links is unfinished.
4. If it came from somewhere, `source::` says where.

## Linking conventions
- Use wikilinks `[[...]]`. They work in both Logseq and Obsidian.
- Use inline `key:: value` properties. This is Logseq's native syntax and Obsidian's Dataview reads it too — the only syntax both tools agree on.
- Tags are search handles, not categories. Categorisation is the job of structure notes.
